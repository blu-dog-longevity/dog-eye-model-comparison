Sources and provenance — BLU Dog eye model comparison

This is the archived August 26, 2026 comparison of existing models on eight public canine-eye photographs. The website publishes the saved results; it does not run inference or accept uploads. Source descriptions and model predictions are not veterinary ground truth.

Models and roles

Name Input and role Original publisher source
Rady YOLO detector Whole photograph → eye boxes. Shared by both Rady classifiers; confidence threshold 0.25. Pinned source
Rady-4 Detected eye crop → EfficientNet-B3 with GeM pooling and squeeze/excitation; cataract, cherry eye, iris atrophy, healthy. Pinned source
Rady-6 Same detector → separate six-output EfficientNet classifier; adds conjunctivitis and glaucoma. Class order is retained in the runner. Pinned source
DogDiseasePredictor Whole photograph → ViT, 20 mixed canine labels. Cherry eye and nuclear sclerosis are the two eye-specific labels. Pinned source
SAM 2.1 Hiera Tiny Boxes and/or positive/negative points → masks. No disease classification. Meta source at the recorded revision
DogEyeSeg4 (not run) A separate 145-image research dataset with masks; not a classifier used in this comparison. Burić et al., 2024

The recovered model runner, classifier driver, and dependency list preserve the implementation. Rady crops use 15% padding, resize to 224 × 224, and ImageNet normalization. DogDiseasePredictor receives the whole image resized to 224 × 224. Scores are model outputs, not calibrated probabilities of disease.

How the outputs were produced

  1. The same eight photographs were supplied to Rady-4, Rady-6, and DogDiseasePredictor. The Rady detector produced nine crop rows per classifier; two additional image rows record detector misses, for 11 rows per Rady sheet. DogDiseasePredictor has eight whole-image rows.
  2. The first SAM pass used the Rady boxes plus manually supplied boxes for the two detector misses. Its 11 masks and descriptors are retained in SAM results, CSV, and the workbook. This pass is not a visible tab in the final August report.
  3. The targeted SAM pass used manual boxes, positive/negative clicks, and manually selected mask candidates for two eyes in the coloboma image and one dark Dalmatian eye. Exact coordinates and candidate choices are in run_targeted_sam.py and results.json. The dark-eye attempt failed to separate pupil from iris, so its pupil/iris metrics were withheld.
  4. The report uses saved classifier JSON, saved SAM JSON, and the images. It does not infer new labels when opened. build_report.py generates the tabbed HTML, CSVs, workbook, and detection-box illustrations.

The targeted area ratio is pupil-mask area divided by iris-disk-mask area after intersecting the pupil with the iris disk. The equivalent-diameter ratio is the square root of that ratio. Shape asymmetry compares a mask with its horizontal reflection. Center offset is normalized by the equivalent iris radius. Color descriptors depend on illumination and camera settings. These quantities are exploratory pixel measurements, not validated clinical measurements. Exact formulas are in run_sam_metrics.py and run_targeted_sam.py.

Known limitations visible in the original results

  • The Rady detector misses two input images, duplicates the visible Dalmatian eye, and detects the nose in another image. The report preserves these cases and marks the duplicate and nose detection.
  • The two Rady classifiers disagree on some crops. Their predictions and confidence scores are retained as produced.
  • DogDiseasePredictor gives diffuse scores on this set and frequently selects a non-eye class. No diagnostic accuracy estimate follows from these examples.
  • SAM candidate selection was manual for the targeted examples. The saved ratios are not automated ground truth or evidence of disease.
  • Eight selected public images do not form an independently labeled clinical benchmark. No sensitivity, specificity, Dice score against expert masks, or external validation was established here.

Archive history and reproducibility

The original classifier source and image manifest were recovered from Git commit d7a2a3cb35939a8a0139b423a3f0f06fbac02d24 of blu-dog-longevity/blu-dog-longevity, under prototypes/canine-eye-pipeline/. Later SAM and report-builder changes were recovered from the recorded August 26 editing session. The saved images and result files survived in the local prototype archive. Private session transcripts and unrelated platform files are not part of this public package.

Before adding publication links, running the recovered builder against the saved results reproduced the original report HTML byte for byte. The original HTML checksum records that comparison target. Publication changes add navigation, image credits, and this source record; the model outputs are unchanged. The archived workbook is retained byte for byte. No model inference was rerun for publication.

SHA256SUMS identifies the published source files, inputs, raw results, derived images, and downloads. asset-lineage.json maps every published image asset to its input photograph, transformation, creator, and license. Hashes identify these retained bytes; they do not establish veterinary ground truth.

For a local static preview, run python -m http.server 8000 from the repository root. To regenerate the presentation from saved outputs, install Pillow and openpyxl and run python pipeline/build_report.py. No model downloads are required for that step. Full inference instructions and the historical dependency pins are in pipeline/README.md. The historical dependency list is not a complete environment lock; exact inference reproducibility on a fresh machine has not been revalidated.
